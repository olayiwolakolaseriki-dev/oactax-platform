5 Most Common Corporate Tax Traps in Nigeria

Over 80% of punitive back-duty penalties issued by the FIRS and State IRS stem from procedural and technical ledger discrepancies that could have been resolved before official audit notice.

Risk 01

Unreconciled WHT Credit Notes

Clients deduct 5% or 10% WHT but fail to remit or issue physical credit note receipts. FIRS disallows the offset on your annual return, resulting in double taxation and penalty demands.

Risk 02

Input VAT Ineligibility

Claiming input VAT on administrative expenses, capital equipment, or overheads rather than raw inventory. Revenue auditors routinely reverse these claims with retroactive 10% penalty plus interest.

Risk 03

Related-Party Management Fees

Intercompany shared costs, parent company royalties, and technical fees without a formal Transfer Pricing Local File. FIRS disallows the entire expense, reclassifying it as taxable operating profit.

Risk 04

Capital Allowance Documentation

Claiming heavy capital allowances on newly acquired plant, machinery, or building assets without Industrial Inspectorate Division (IID) Acceptance Certificates.

Risk 05

State PAYE & Expatriate Returns

Discrepancies between audited staff payroll costs, pension remittances, and annual Form H1 filings. State Internal Revenue Services (such as LIRS) impose severe best-of-judgment liabilities.
